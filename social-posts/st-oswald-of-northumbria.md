Before the battle that would decide whether Christianity survived in northern England, a young exiled king raised a wooden cross with his own hands and asked his soldiers to kneel and pray with him.

Oswald had spent his youth in exile among Irish monks, and when he returned to reclaim Northumbria at the Battle of Heavenfield in 634, he brought their faith back with him. He went on to reunite a divided kingdom and invited missionaries to establish Christianity across the north — before dying in battle himself just eight years later. He's remembered today as a soldier-saint and martyr.

Today is his feast day.
