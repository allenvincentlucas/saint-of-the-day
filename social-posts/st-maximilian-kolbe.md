A guard demanded to know why a prisoner had stepped out of line. "I am a Catholic priest," he said. "I want to take his place."

Maximilian Kolbe was a Polish Franciscan who built the largest friary in the world around a printing press, spreading devotion to Mary to over a million readers before sheltering thousands of refugees — most of them Jewish — from the Nazis. Arrested and sent to Auschwitz in 1941, he volunteered to die in a starvation bunker in place of a stranger who'd cried out for his wife and children. He was the last of the ten to die, two weeks later. That man, Franciszek Gajowniczek, lived another 54 years and stood in St. Peter's Square when Kolbe was canonized.

Today is his feast day.
