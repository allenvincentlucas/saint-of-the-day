She sold purple cloth to the wealthiest households in Thyatira — and became the first recorded convert to Christianity on European soil.

Lydia was a businesswoman of real independent means, likely a widow or a former slave who'd built her own trade in the rare and expensive purple dye reserved for royalty and the elite. When Paul preached by a riverbank in Philippi, Scripture says simply that "the Lord opened her heart" — and she opened her home, becoming the anchor of the first Christian community in Europe.

Her feast day varies by tradition: August 3, May 20, January 27, or October 25, depending on the calendar.
