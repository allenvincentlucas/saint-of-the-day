He was seated in his bishop's chair, mid-homily, when the soldiers broke in.

Pope Sixtus II led Rome's Christians through a year of the emperor Valerian's persecution — worshipping in secret, in the catacombs outside the city walls — until soldiers caught him doing exactly what the edict forbade: celebrating Mass. He was beheaded within the hour. His deacon Lawrence would follow four days later. Sixtus was buried in the Crypt of the Popes, and within a few centuries his name was written into the Roman Canon — the oldest Eucharistic Prayer of the Mass — where it's still spoken today, over 1,700 years on.

Today is his feast day.
