She read St. Teresa of Ávila's autobiography in one night and knew, by morning, that she'd found the truth she'd spent years as a philosopher looking for.

Edith Stein was a Jewish phenomenologist who became a Carmelite nun, taking the name Teresa Benedicta of the Cross. When the Nazis intensified their persecution of Jews in 1942, she was arrested along with her sister and deported to Auschwitz, where she died. In 1999, she was named a co-patroness of Europe — one of the only Jewish-born converts ever given that title.

Today is her feast day.
