He struggled so badly in seminary that he nearly wasn't ordained — then spent up to 16 hours a day, for over 40 years, hearing the confessions of people who traveled from across France just to sit with him.

John Vianney was sent to Ars-sur-Formans, a small, spiritually neglected French village nobody else wanted, and turned it into a place of pilgrimage. He wasn't a scholar or a great preacher by his own admission — he was simply, relentlessly present to anyone who came looking for God. That became his entire ministry, and the reason he's remembered as the patron of parish priests.

Today is his feast day.
