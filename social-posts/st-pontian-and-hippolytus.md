History's first antipope spent eighteen years insisting the real pope of Rome was a fraud. Then they ended up in the same mine.

Hippolytus broke from the Church over how strictly it should treat sinners, setting himself up as a rival bishop of Rome against three successive popes, including Pontian. In 235 AD, both men were exiled to hard labor in Sardinia during a Roman persecution — and somewhere in that suffering, the schism simply ended. Pontian had already resigned the papacy so the Church wouldn't be left without a leader; both men died in the mines that year, reconciled. Their bodies were later buried together in Rome.

Today is their feast day.
