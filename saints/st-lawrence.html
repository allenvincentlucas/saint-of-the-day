SAINT OF THE DAY · 3RD CENTURY

St. Lawrence

Ordered to hand over the Church's wealth, he spent three days giving it to the poor, then presented the poor themselves as the treasure — and was executed for the joke.

Feast August 10 Deacon & Martyr, Rome Patron of the Poor, Cooks & Deacons

A Deacon in Sixtus's Rome

Second in Command of Nothing Small

Lawrence was likely born around 225 in the region of Huesca, in what is now Spain, and came to Rome to study under the man who would become Pope Sixtus II.

When Sixtus was elected pope in 257, he ordained Lawrence a deacon and made him first among Rome's seven deacons — the archdeacon, responsible for the Church's material property and its distribution of alms to widows, orphans, and the poor. It was a position of real financial trust in a Church that, by the mid-3rd century, held meaningful wealth.

That trust was tested almost immediately. In 258, the emperor Valerian escalated his persecution of Christians with an edict ordering the immediate execution of bishops, priests, and deacons. On 6 August, Sixtus and several of his fellow deacons were seized while celebrating Mass in a Roman cemetery and put to death the same day. Lawrence, according to a story recorded a century later by St. Ambrose of Milan, ran after his bishop as he was led away and asked why he was being left behind. Sixtus is remembered telling him he would follow in a few days, with a greater trial still to come.

The Treasures of the Church

Three Days to Gather the Gold

The Roman prefect knew Lawrence controlled the Church's funds, and offered him a deal: hand over the treasury, and go free.

Lawrence asked for three days to collect it all together. He spent them doing something else entirely — distributing as much of the Church's money as he could reach directly into the hands of Rome's poor, sick, and disabled. On the third day, he returned to the prefect with a crowd of the city's beggars, cripples, and orphans in tow, and presented them as the treasure he had promised.

ST. AMBROSE OF MILAN, DE OFFICIIS, BOOK II (4TH CENTURY)
“Behold in these poor persons the treasures which I promised to show you; to which I will add pearls and precious stones, those widows and consecrated virgins, which are the Church's crown.”
Public domain English rendering of Ambrose's account, written roughly a century after Lawrence's death

Ambrose's account, written well after the fact, is the earliest detailed record of this story, and historians treat it as a cherished tradition rather than a contemporary transcript — the earliest, near-contemporary sources simply record that Sixtus's clergy were executed, without singling out this episode. Whether the exchange happened in these exact words or not, it became, and remains, the story that defines Lawrence's memory: wealth measured in people, not coin.

Turned Into Legend

The Emblem That Outgrew the Story

The enraged prefect is said to have ordered Lawrence executed by being roasted alive on a gridiron over slow coals — on 10 August 258, four days after Sixtus.

Tradition adds one more detail on top of the horror of it: that partway through his execution, Lawrence turned to his tormentors and said, in effect, “I'm well done on this side — turn me over.” It is one of the most famous lines attributed to any early martyr, and almost certainly legendary rather than historical.

Some scholars, including the Bollandist historian Hippolyte Delehaye, have argued that the gridiron account may not be accurate at all — the earliest testimony suggests Sixtus's deacons, Lawrence likely among them, were simply beheaded on the day of their arrest, the same as their bishop. On this reading, the vivid gridiron narrative developed later, possibly absorbing details from a different persecution altogether. The tradition is centuries old and deeply woven into how Lawrence has been remembered and depicted ever since — but it sits alongside real uncertainty about exactly how he died.

Second Only to Peter and Paul

However he died, Lawrence became one of the most venerated martyrs of the early Roman Church — ancient tradition ranked his popularity in the city second only to the apostles Peter and Paul. The Basilica di San Lorenzo fuori le Mura, one of Rome's seven pilgrim churches, was built over his tomb. His emblem, the gridiron, gave him an unusual and slightly wry family of patronages: cooks, chefs, comedians, and firefighters, alongside the poor and his fellow deacons. Even the Perseid meteor shower, which peaks each year around his August 10 feast, was folded into his story — medieval tradition called the falling stars “the tears of St. Lawrence.”

Prayer

For His Intercession

Saint Lawrence, you looked at gold and looked at people and knew immediately which one was worth more. Teach us to measure our own treasure the way you measured yours — in who we have fed, sheltered, and stood beside, not in what we have kept for ourselves. Pray for deacons, for the poor, and for everyone who serves quietly with what has been entrusted to them. Amen.
